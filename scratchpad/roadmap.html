Honesty guardrails — what would tempt a lie, and the rule that stops it
  • Look-ahead: never score or resolve against an in-progress bar (N7); calibration and every model must be walk-forward with a strict ts-cutoff (N1, L2). If it grades on data it was fit on, it's a lie.
  • Overfitting: no model, weight, or feature enters the ensemble without positive out-of-sample Lift (X6, L3). Shrink toward the prior until evidence earns the tilt (N6). No neural net under ~5k resolved outcomes.
  • Fake / thin data: IEX = ~2–3% of volume — label it as such until SIP (X3). Prices are price-return until adjustment=all (N4). A proxy is always flagged as a proxy; never fabricated.
  • Silent corruption: a split must raise a DQ event, not rewrite permanent history (L1); a gap must be detected, not hidden by a passing row-count (X4).
  • Show the error bar: every shipped probability carries its uncertainty and its gate state (X9). Adaptive weights stay available:false until n≥30 — never imply edge before resolved outcomes exist.
▲ TradingView — can you pull data from it?

NO — verified on TradingView's own support page. "We don't have an API that gives access to data… our REST API is meant for brokers." TradingView sells charting front-ends (the OSS lightweight-charts you already use correctly) and broker order-routing — neither emits redistributable market data.

The reseller tradingviewapi.com and libs tvdatafeed / tradingview-ta reverse-engineer TV's private websocket via a logged-in session: ToS-violating, ban-prone, unmaintained (tvdatafeed: no commits since Sep 2022), and — for tradingview-ta — an un-auditable Buy/Sell gauge that breaks the honesty doctrine outright.

Verdict: keep lightweight-charts as your renderer (licensed, correct — see X2). Get data from a real vendor below. TradingView is a charting/social layer, not a data source.
